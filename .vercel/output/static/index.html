One small swap: higher-protein day vs lower-protein day

Protein tends to help people feel full longer, so many end up eating a bit less without trying. Slide forward to see how that could add up.

Rough illustration only, not a prediction. In one study, people who raised protein from about 15% to 30% of their calories ate about 441 fewer calories a day on their own.1 To be careful, this uses a much smaller 150 calories a day, and roughly 3,500 calories ≈ 1 lb of fat. Results vary from person to person and are often smaller. Not medical advice.

1. Weigelt DS, et al. A high-protein diet induces sustained reductions in appetite, ad libitum caloric intake, and body weight. Am J Clin Nutr. 2005;82(1):41–48.
